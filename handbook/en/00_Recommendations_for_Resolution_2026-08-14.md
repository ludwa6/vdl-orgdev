## 0. The two rules everything else follows from

**R1 — One language per document.** The Portuguese set carries no English; the English set carries
no Portuguese. This is the rule that resolves most of section B, and it has one consequence worth
being clear about: **the English terms disappear from the Portuguese documents entirely.** No more
*Domínios (Domains)*, no more *Líder do Círculo (Circle Lead)*.

**R2 — Proper names are the exception, and there is a fixed list of them.** These stay as written
in both languages, because they name a thing rather than describe one:

> **Vale da Lama** · **Quinta Vale da Lama** · **Casa** · **Lama Village** · **Holacracy®** ·
> **ama** (the restaurant — lowercase, never translated)
>
> *Corrected 2026-08-15:* this list previously named *"Da Horta ao Prato… e do Prato à Horta"* as the
> restaurant. It is not — it is the **motto**. The restaurant is **ama**, and the motto is translated
> in the English set like any other prose.

Anything not on that list gets translated. If a term feels like it belongs on the list, the answer
is to add it here deliberately, not to leave it mixed in the text.

**R3 — One bilingual crosswalk, in one place.** Losing the English parentheticals costs something
real: a Portuguese reader can no longer connect *Líder do Círculo* to the Holacracy literature. The
fix is a single **bilingual terminology table at the end of the glossary (doc 09)**, in both
language sets, clearly labelled as a crosswalk. One page where both languages appear on purpose,
so that nowhere else has to.
