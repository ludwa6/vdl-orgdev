# The Secretary's Manual

**For whoever fills the Secretary Role in a Vale da Lama Circle.**

Document 04 of the Governance Handbook says *what* the Secretary is accountable for. This says *how
it is done*, step by step, with the places each thing gets written.

> **English translation.** The Portuguese version at
> [`docs/pt/manual-do-secretario.md`](../pt/manual-do-secretario.md) is the one that counts. Where
> the two disagree, the Portuguese governs — the same rule the Handbook itself follows.

> **Status: proposal.** Written 2026-08-20, not yet adopted in a Governance Meeting. It serves as a
> working guide and for training; it becomes binding when the Circle adopts it. If anything here
> contradicts the Handbook, the Handbook wins — and that is a defect in this document, to be fixed.

---

## 1. What the Role is, and what it is not

The Secretary is **not the person who takes notes.** The Secretary stabilises the records and
meetings the Governance System requires of the Circle, and **interprets the Governance System** when
asked.

When interpreting, read our sources of governance in this order:

1. **The Governance Handbook** and the Policies adopted by the Circles.
2. **The Register of Adopted Variations** — document 05.
3. **The Holacracy Constitution, version 5.0** — for everything the first two do not resolve.

Your ruling binds everyone until the text or the context changes. If you find a divergence between
the Handbook and the Constitution that is **not** in the Register of Adopted Variations, **that is
not a new rule: it is a defect.** Flag it to the Circle. It is resolved by amending the Handbook, or
by adopting the variation in a Governance Meeting and entering it in the Register.

**Your Domain is the Circle's governance records.** Only Secretaries write to them. Everyone reads
them. There is one deliberate exception: **anyone may raise a Tension, at any time, without asking
anyone's permission** — and that includes a Tension about your work as Secretary.

How the Role is filled: elected by the members of the Circle, through the Integrative Election
Process. Whoever holds the Circle Lead Role may not be Secretary of the same Circle.

## 2. The cycle of a Governance Meeting

### Before

1. **Schedule** the meeting and give all Circle members due notice.
2. If anyone requests a special Governance Meeting, **schedule it**. That is not a discretion of
   yours: it is an Accountability.

You do not need to prepare an agenda. A Governance Meeting's agenda **is built from the participants
during the meeting** — and it is the Facilitator who runs it, not the Secretary.

### During

3. **Record what is adopted**, proposal by proposal. Enough to write the entry afterwards: what was
   proposed, by whom, and what was decided.
4. If you are asked for an interpretation, **give it** — and note that you did, because it goes in
   the Decision Log.

### After — **within two days, and always before that Circle's next meeting**

5. **Write one Decision Log entry per adopted proposal.**
6. **Update Notion** so the structure matches what was decided — Roles, Accountabilities, Domains,
   Policies. See §4.
7. **Publish to the Circle** that it is done.

> The two-day target is **our own discipline, not a rule of the Constitution**. A governance
> decision takes effect **when the Circle adopts it**, not when it is logged. If the log runs late,
> the decision still stands — what is lost is the memory of why.

---

## 3. The Decision Log — what goes in it

Four kinds of entry, and nothing else:

| Kind | What you write |
|---|---|
| **Governance** | The adopted proposals: Roles created, changed or removed; Domains; Accountabilities; Policies |
| **Elections** | Who now holds Facilitator, Secretary or Circle Rep, and for what term |
| **Interpretations** | Your rulings on how the Governance System is to be read, binding on everyone until the text or context changes |
| **Domain permissions** | When someone grants or refuses permission over a Domain they hold |

On the last one: **the person who holds the Domain tells you; you log it.** One message is enough.
**Refusals are logged just as approvals are** — a refusal is a decision, and its reasoning is
exactly what is needed when the question comes back six months later.

A useful entry answers four questions: **what, who, when, and why.** The *why* is the one most
missed later and the first to be forgotten.

## 4. Where each thing gets written

There are two places, and the line between them is firm:

| | **Notion** | **The GitHub repository** |
|---|---|---|
| Holds | The **structure**: Circles, Roles, Domains, Accountabilities, Policies, who fills what, Tensions, Meetings | The **Handbook**: Purpose, Values, how we work, the glossary |
| Changes | At every Governance Meeting | Rarely, and by decision |
| Who writes | The Secretaries | Whoever proposes a change to the Handbook |

**The practical rule:** if the change is about *who does what* or *what Roles exist*, it is
**Notion**. If it is about *how we describe ourselves* or *how we work*, it is the **Handbook**.

Almost all of a Secretary's routine work is Notion. The Handbook changes a few times a year.

### If the decision changes the Handbook

The Handbook is Markdown, in `handbook/pt/`. **That is the version that counts** — the `.docx` and
`.pages` files are generated from it, and a change made in those is lost.

Three routes, all valid:

1. **Open an Issue** in the repository saying what has to change and why. It is the simplest route
   and requires nothing else.
2. **Edit directly on GitHub** — open the file, click the pencil, write, save. GitHub keeps the
   change as a proposal until someone accepts it.
3. **Ask someone who knows how.** Not a lesser step: it is how the August 2026 revision was made.

What does **not** work is editing a `.docx` or a `.pages`.

## 5. When the Role is unfilled

If the Facilitator or the Secretary is unfilled or unavailable, **a surrogate may act in the Role**.
By default the surrogate for the Facilitator is the acting Secretary, and the surrogate for the
Secretary is the acting Facilitator.

This is not a permanent arrangement. An elected Role that falls vacant **should go to election** —
and it is the Facilitator who triggers an election when a term ends. Any member may call for an
election at any time, without waiting for the end of anything.

## 6. The questions that always come up

**"Is this a Governance Meeting or a Tactical Meeting?"**
Governance changes the *structure* — Roles, Domains, Accountabilities, Policies. Tactical unblocks
the *work* inside the structure that already exists. If nobody wants to change who is accountable
for what, it is Tactical.

**"Does this really have to be a governance proposal?"**
Often not. If someone has authority over the Domain, they decide and that is that — no meeting, no
consensus needed. Governance exists to *clarify who decides*, not to decide everything.

**"Someone disagrees with the record I wrote."**
That is a Tension, and anyone may raise it. The record is your Domain, but a Domain is exclusive
control over the *writing*, not immunity from discussion.

**"I found something in the Handbook that is wrong."**
Good. Open an Issue. A Handbook nobody corrects is a Handbook nobody is reading.

**"I don't know how to use GitHub."**
You do not need to in order to fill this Role. You need to know how to use Notion. GitHub comes up a
few times a year, and route 1 in §4 — open an Issue — covers most cases.
